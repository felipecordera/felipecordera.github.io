<p class="intro-label">Operations Research · MIT</p>

I am a PhD candidate at MIT’s Operations Research Center, advised by Alexandre Jacquillat and Cynthia Barnhart. I develop optimization models and algorithms for large-scale operational systems, balancing model fidelity and computational scalability.

My research focuses on how to represent complex system interactions and make decisions when important information is uncertain or unavailable. I develop solution methods that preserve the features that matter for decision quality while keeping optimization tractable at realistic scale. My work spans robotic fulfillment, advanced air mobility, airport operations, and energy systems.

## Research directions

**Representing complex operational interactions.** I study how optimization models can capture the downstream consequences of operational decisions without representing every detail of the system. I am interested in when these effects should be modeled jointly, approximated, or handled through separate decision layers.

**Decision-making with incomplete information.** My work examines how uncertainty and unavailable information affect operational and planning decisions. A central question is what information matters for decision quality, and how it can be represented or inferred within a tractable optimization framework.

**Learning from decisions.** Looking ahead, I am interested in how observed decisions can reveal the underlying objectives of the agents making them, and how this information can inform system-level optimization. A central question is how much needs to be learned about these objectives to support effective decisions.

## Background

Before MIT, I studied civil engineering with a concentration in environmental and water resources at Universidad de Chile, where I worked on stochastic optimization in power systems and water resources under the guidance of Marcelo Olivares, Fernando Ordóñez, and Rodrigo Moreno.

During my PhD at MIT, I spent summer 2025 as a Research Scientist II Intern at Amazon Robotics. I expect to complete my PhD in May 2027.

My work on unit commitment under correlated renewable uncertainty received the **2024 ENRE Student Best Paper Award**.
