I want students to develop the judgment to decide what belongs in a mathematical model, what can be simplified, and how to evaluate a solution beyond the model that produced it.

In recitations and office hours, I begin with a problem and invite students to propose approaches before developing the method. I use concrete examples to motivate abstract ideas and adapt explanations to students with different mathematical backgrounds.

Teaching interests

I am prepared to teach linear, integer, and network optimization; stochastic and robust optimization; and large-scale optimization methods at the master’s or doctoral level.

I am also interested in developing courses on inverse optimization and learning from decisions, and on optimization for water resources and energy systems.

Advising

I aim to provide a supportive, inquisitive research environment in which students develop intellectual ownership, make meaningful choices about their work, and contribute ideas through collaboration. My experience includes working with undergraduate and master’s students on research projects.
